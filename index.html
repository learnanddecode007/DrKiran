On the Existence and Uniqueness of Kiran

by everyone who has ever borrowed something from her desk

Abstract. We prove that for every friend f in our group, there exists a Kiran (recognizable by her gorgeous curly hair, killer fashion sense, and an endless list of talents) such that f has, at some point, been fed, driven around, rescued, entertained, or taught a card game by her. She sings like a dream, drives like she's been doing it forever, dresses like every day is a Pinterest board, and somehow owns all the cutest stationery known to mankind. Her talents seem endless, and neither does her collection of cute things. She can make an ordinary day fun, a random moment memorable, and a room feel a little more alive just by being in it. The proof is long, joyful, occasionally chaotic, and slightly illegal for Room No. 28, Block 4. Written by Ziwani (with a lot of love and an unreasonable number of tissues).

The Journey

every great theorem has a long proof

The PhD Era

Four years ago, Kiran decided to pursue a PhD in ODE and Numerical Analysis under Sachin Sir's supervision (despite excessive oil usage). What followed was a series of equations, MATLAB codes, research papers, revisions, and probably more questions than anyone had originally signed up for. There were days when the equations refused to cooperate, papers refused to get accepted, and Sachin Sir's expectations refused to become any easier. But here she is, four years later, finally getting that Dr. before her name XD.

The problem was difficult; the iterations were many, but Kiran never stopped trying. Convergence achieved!

The Multitasking Lemma (How Many Problems Can One Person Handle?)

A normal person might struggle to manage work and personal life together. Kiran, however, decided to take up research, teaching, responsibilities, personal commitments, Stanley, cute key chains,  and approximately seventeen other things at the same time. And somehow, she still manages to show up for people around her.

We tried reducing the number of variables, but she insisted on solving the entire system together. No complaints, just Kiran things.

The Teaching Theorem (Students Have Spoken!)

Being good at mathematics is one thing. Making other people understand it is a completely different game. From being a research scholar to teaching at NSUT, Kiran has managed to win over her students, too. And if anyone has doubts, Gajendra Purohit's YouTube comment section is right there to settle the matter.

Most of her students are her followers!! 

The Kiran Has Too Many Talents Theorem

Now, coming to the part where mathematics starts falling short. This woman can sing beautifully enough to make you stop whatever you're doing and listen. Her curls have their own personality, her fashion sense deserves a Pinterest board, and her driving skills make her the obvious choice for the next road trip. She can teach you swimming, introduce you to card games and somehow always have the cutest stationery around. We are yet to discover the complete list of her talents.

We attempted to count them. The enumeration is still incomplete.

The Final Theorem (Dr. Kiran!)

After four years of research, countless equations, papers, deadlines, revisions, and everything else that came along with the journey, we finally arrive at the result we have all been waiting for.


Kiran = Dr. Kiran

No approximations required. No further iterations needed. Result accepted!

Q.E.D.

Lemmas of Friendship

all proven. no peer review needed.

Lemma 1. Any problem, from a missing pen to a headache, has a solution inside Kiran’s desk.
Lemma 2. If a card game exists in our group, Kiran introduced it (and probably won).
Lemma 3. Every road, near or far, is shorter in Kiran’s car.
Lemma 4. The number of tissues in her desk is never zero. It is a constant of nature.
Lemma 5. Birthday + Kiran = party. Always. No exceptions.
Corollary. Therefore, Kiran is the best friend. ∎
